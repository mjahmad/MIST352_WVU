SLIDE 3 — DATA SOURCE: WHERE THE FORECAST COMES FROM
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Source: NOAA / National Weather Service Forecast Data
Free public weather data — no API key required.

HOW IT WORKS — 3 STEPS:

Step 1  User enters a location (example: "Morgantown, WV")
↓
The app retrieves local forecast conditions for that area.

Step 2  Forecast includes:
temperature, wind speed, wind direction,

Step 3  The app converts forecast data into actual bonfire decisions:
Should you have a fire tonight?

Where will the smoke go?

WHAT WE EXTRACT:
· High Temp (°F)        · Low Temp (°F)
· Wind Speed (mph)      · Wind Direction
· Precip Chance (%)     · Humidity (%)
· Forecast Description  · Fire Readiness
· Smoke Direction       · Best Seating Recommendation

SLIDE 4 — WHY WIND DIRECTION MATTERS
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Most people only check if it will rain.

The real comfort problem at a bonfire is usually smoke.

Wind direction determines:
· where smoke travels
· where people should avoid sitting
· where the best seating locations are

EXAMPLE:

If wind is blowing FROM North TO South:

· smoke drifts South
· sitting South puts you directly in the smoke
· the best seating is East or West

This makes the project more realistic than simply checking weather.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SLIDE 5 — HOW THE SEATING LOGIC WORKS
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
The project uses TWO separate methods:

GetOpposite()
· Determines where the smoke drifts

Example:
Wind from North → Smoke goes South

GetBestDirection()
· Determines where users should sit

Example:
Wind from North → Sit East or West

WHY THIS MATTERS:

Returning only the opposite direction would place users directly
inside the smoke path.

The safer recommendation is always the adjacent / perpendicular side.
